# Writing for a person

Use this before creating or revising any Notion page or database row, and when
handing a piece of work back in the conversation. It applies wherever Notion is
connected, including pages outside the context home. It does not authorize a
write. Destination, retention and authority still come from the current task
and the recorded write policy.

Outward-facing prose, such as a message, email, post or other text that goes
out under the author's name, stays with
[Human Writing](../../osm-human-writing/SKILL.md). This page does not replace
that method, and writing a draft does not authorize sending or publishing it.

## Match the existing format first

Read the destination before choosing a shape.

- For a database, read its schema and properties, its template when one
  exists, and one or two recent rows about a comparable kind of thing.
- For a page area that is not a database, read the parent and one or two
  nearby pages that serve the same purpose.
- Mirror their structure, names, property use, section order and length.
  Fill the properties those rows already use. Do not add a section, property
  or report layout when the template or the recent pages already show the
  shape.
- When the destination is empty and has no template, keep the page to the
  result, why it is so, and what is still open. Do not invent a report
  structure for a single record.
- A managed home record still follows the
  [record lifecycle](../../osm-data-context/references/architecture.md#record-lifecycle):
  one current record, a stable identity, and a concise explanation and Summary.
  Revise that record in place.
- If the destination or the authority is uncertain, hold the proposed page
  with the task.

Match length as well as headings. A short decision stays about as short as
the recent decisions around it. A guide, article or other page that this
destination already treats as a long document may stay long. Do not compress
that guide into a status note, and do not stretch a short record into a guide.

## Say the useful part first

Start with what the reader needs: the outcome, the finding or the decision.
Use the shortest form that keeps the meaning, the material uncertainty and
the evidence the claim depends on. A routine note is often a short summary
and the next step.

Write in plain language a person can scan. Use the same terms the existing
pages use. Put deeper evidence behind a toggle, a link or a later section
when the recent pages do. Keep an important limitation or a negative finding
where the reader will see it.

Leave out a log of the steps taken, a file-by-file inventory, a chronology of
the session and a conclusion repeated in several sections. Include that
material only when the reader asked for it or the destination's existing
pages already include it.

## In the reply

The reply that accompanies a page, or that closes a piece of work, uses the
same shape. Lead with the useful point, then only what the person needs next.
Name a saved or held page and where it went. An ordinary checkpoint that
changed nothing stays quiet. Do not paste the page body, or a transcript of
the tool calls, into the chat.
