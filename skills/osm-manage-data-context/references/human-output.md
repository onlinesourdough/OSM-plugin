# Writing for a person

Use this when writing or revising an authorised Notion page or row and its
accompanying reply, including outside the context home. The task's authority, source retention
and verification rules still apply. This grants no read or write permission.
Source-only and restricted originals stay at their owner; do not copy,
paraphrase or summarise them into Notion to bypass permitted processing or retention.

Outward-facing prose, such as a message, email, post or other text that goes
out under the author's name, stays with [Human Writing](../../osm-human-writing/SKILL.md),
even when stored in Notion. This reference governs its surrounding properties,
template and handback, not the prose body's voice or approved wording. Drafting
does not authorise sending or publishing.

## Match the existing format first

Reuse verified context; refresh affected facts when scope, access or content
changes. Read the destination before changing it.

- For a small in-place edit, use the target's existing structure. Inspect schema
  if needed for unverified properties; no template or neighbour reads by default.
- For a new page or requested reshape, inspect the relevant schema/template.
  Only if the format is still unclear, read up to two comparable examples within
  the task's authorised scope. Skip unrelated, other-client or restricted pages;
  connector access alone is not authority to read them. Without permitted examples,
  use the template/schema or a minimal result, reason and open question.
- Match useful structure, names, properties and section order. Fill only known,
  authorised values; unknown stays unknown. Add no competing schema or report
  layout merely to record one change.
- Preserve useful owner detail, provenance, dated reasons and material failure,
  retry or intervention evidence. Do not overwrite truncated or unknown content.
  Prefer a supported targeted change; request the missing content if needed.
- A managed home record still follows the
  [record lifecycle](../../osm-data-context/references/architecture.md#record-lifecycle):
  one current record, a stable identity, and a concise explanation and Summary.
  Revise that record in place.
- If the destination or the authority is uncertain, hold the proposed page
  with the task.

Match useful length: a short decision stays short; a long guide may stay long.
Do not imitate bloated agent logs or broken recent examples. The selected root
template and schema govern new home structures; an incidental neighbour does
not replace the AI operating model title, definition-first layout or approved icons.

## Say the useful part first

Start with what the reader needs: the outcome, the finding or the decision.
Use the shortest form that keeps the meaning, the material uncertainty and
the evidence the claim depends on. A routine note is often a short summary
and the next step.

Write in plain language a person can scan. Use the same terms the existing
pages use. Put deeper evidence behind a toggle, a link or a later section
when the destination's existing format does. Keep an important limitation or a negative finding
where the reader will see it.

Leave out a log of the steps taken, a file-by-file inventory and repeated
conclusions unless the task needs them, such as a requested audit or incident
timeline. Existing verbosity alone is no reason to copy it or delete useful history.

## In the reply

The reply accompanying that Notion work uses the same shape. Respect the user's
requested format. Lead with the useful point, then what the person needs next.
Name a saved or held page and where it went. An ordinary checkpoint that
changed nothing stays quiet. Do not dump the full page or tool-call transcript.
For a proposal or provisional snapshot, show the concrete summary needed to
approve or correct it, with material checks, failures, limits and open gaps.
